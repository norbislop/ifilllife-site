A blank page can be easier to work with than a crowded to-do list. Give yourself a few quiet minutes and write down one thing you want to understand better.

It does not have to be your biggest problem. It only needs to be specific enough to explore.

## Make the question smaller

“What should I do next?” can lead in a hundred directions. Try something you can actually investigate:

- What part of my week feels unnecessarily difficult?
- What am I assuming that I could check?
- What would I like to learn before I make this choice?

Choose the question that feels useful today. Leave the others on the page for another time.

## Separate what you know from what you’re guessing

Make two short lists: **what I know** and **what I still need to find out**. Be honest about which list each thought belongs on. An assumption can be a useful starting point without being a fact.

You might discover that the next step is a conversation, a document to read, or simply time to think.

## Pick a small, reversible next step

Choose something that helps you learn without requiring a big commitment. Write a rough outline. Ask a clear question. Try a different routine for an afternoon.

When you finish, ask: **What do I understand now that I did not understand before?**

That is enough for a beginning.
